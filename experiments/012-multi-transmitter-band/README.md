# Experiment 012 — which FSK pair survives every transmitter

**Status: in progress. Two transmitter classes measured, a third (Android) outstanding.**

LAB / EXPERIMENTAL. Nothing here is conformance code, and no result here has
changed AP-BOOTSTRAP-1 yet.

## The question

AP-BOOTSTRAP-1 specifies 3000 Hz for bit 0 and 6000 Hz for bit 1. That pair was
chosen by measuring **one** transmitter — the DFR1154's amplifier and speaker —
and it is a good pair for that transmitter. Experiment 002 measured a notch
around 3 kHz in this laptop's speaker, and 3 kHz is one of the two tones.

A profile whose bit-0 tone lands in a common loudspeaker's null is a profile
that its second implementer will report as broken. The profile cannot become
Stable until the band has been chosen against more than one transmitter, and
this experiment is that measurement.

## Method

One emission per transmitter. The transmitter emits a marker tone, a gap, then
27 tones of nominally 100 ms each — 1200 Hz to 9000 Hz in 300 Hz steps —
streamed continuously. The whole response curve comes out of a single burst;
the alternative, a frame per candidate band, is 27 emissions for the same
answer, in a room with people in it.

Candidates are multiples of 300 Hz because 300 baud at 48 kHz is 160 samples
per symbol, and only a multiple of 300 Hz puts a whole number of cycles in a
symbol. A candidate the modem could never adopt is not worth measuring.

The board generates the ladder in firmware (`SWEEP`, firmware v5). Every other
transmitter plays `ladder.wav`, generated by `band_sweep ladder` from the same
constants. The self-test checks the two agree.

## Selection: minimax on SNR

The score is the **weaker of the two tones, on the worst path**, and the winner
maximises it.

Not the average: averaging rewards a band that is superb on one transmitter and
inaudible on another, because a large number and a small number average to an
acceptable one. Bootstrap contact does not average — a stranger arrives on one
path, and if that path cannot carry the tone, contact does not happen.

Not raw level, either. Two transmitters cannot be driven to the same absolute
level by a rig: a board's digital emission gain and a laptop's render volume
are different knobs, and comparing dBFS across them ranks the knobs. SNR
against each path's own measured room floor has the property this needs —
capture gain multiplies tone and room alike and cancels exactly, while a
transmitter that genuinely cannot drive its speaker still scores as weak. The
floor is measured in each capture's own pre-roll.

## Result so far

Both paths recorded 2026-09-06, capture gain 0.12, one burst each.

The selection scores the **preamble chirp as well as the two FSK tones**.
AP-BOOTSTRAP-1 derives the acquisition chirp from the pair -- it sweeps from
`f0 - 1000` up to `f1` -- so scoring only the tones scores the data and ignores
the thing that has to find the data.

| pair | worst-path weaker-tone SNR |
|---|---|
| **6000 / 7200 Hz** (minimax winner) | **55.75 dB** |
| 3000 / 6000 Hz (incumbent), scored the same way | 32.64 dB |

The incumbent scores badly on its own preamble: its chirp sweeps 2000-6000 Hz,
straight through the laptop speaker's measured 3 kHz notch.

**No band change is proposed on this evidence, and the number above has not
been confirmed over air.** See the retraction below.

## Retracted: the first two over-air trials measured nothing

Two cells were run at candidate bands, 1500/6300 and then 6000/7200. Both came
back `acquired=0` with correlation ~0.10 at healthy peaks (-10.5 dB, -8.9 dB).

The first was written up here as evidence that a chirp starting at 500 Hz is
below what the transmitter can drive. **That was wrong.** Experiment 011's rig
applied `-Band` to the host tool only -- `gen`, `hex` and `decode` -- and never
sent `BAND` to the board. The board emitted at the modem default while the host
decoded a different band. Correlation ~0.10 is what a transmit/receive band
mismatch looks like, and it says nothing whatever about either candidate.

The rig now sends `BAND` and treats a refusal as fatal, because a rig that
cannot set the band it claims to be testing produces numbers about a different
band.

The chirp rule is kept in the selection, but on ARGUMENT rather than on that
run: the receiver correlates against the derived chirp, so a chirp sweeping
where the transmitter cannot drive costs acquisition however clean the two
tones are. It has not been demonstrated over air.

**Outstanding, and none of it is startable without a fresh emission budget:**

* re-run 1500/6300 and 6000/7200 with the repaired rig, which is the only way
  to know whether either candidate is real;
* the 10 B / 16 B / 17 B cells on whichever pair survives;
* the Android transmitter class, which is the second INDEPENDENT class the
  Stable criterion asks for.

Two transmitter classes is one more than one, and still not enough.

## What the instrument refuses to do

`slots` reports, for every tone, the strongest that frequency read in any
**other** slot. A tone that reads weaker inside its own slot than outside it is
not a quiet tone, it is a misaligned measurement, and when a majority do so the
tool rejects the whole curve rather than printing levels that describe some
other part of the recording.

That check earned its place on the first over-air run. See below.

## Finding: the board's SWEEP emission is 1.28x long, and the modem's is not

The first board capture came back with every tone weaker in its own slot than
outside it — an impossibility, so the curve was rejected rather than
interpreted. The capture showed why: the tones were at exactly the right
frequencies (6000 Hz measured to 20 Hz resolution) but each slot lasted 128 ms
instead of 100.

Correct pitch with stretched duration is not a clock error — a slow clock would
drop 6000 Hz to 4800. It is the board emitting more samples than the firmware
generated, in its I2S path, under the new `SWEEP` code.

Two independent checks bound the finding:

* The **laptop** playing the identical ladder fits at **1.0079** — nominal. So
  the stretch is the board, not the analyser.
* Archived board emissions from experiment 011 decode with the shipped receiver
  at **sps 159.75–160.10 against a nominal 160**, correlation 0.85+. The
  receiver's whole-frame refinement searches ±0.6 samples per symbol out of
  160, or ±0.375%; a 28% error could not decode at all. **The board's modem
  emissions are at the correct rate and the archived E4 evidence is
  unaffected.**

The defect is in this experiment's new tone generator and is confined to it.

It is also why the analyser no longer trusts the transmitter's timing. It fits
the slot start and period from the capture — least squares on each tone's own
detected peak, seeded with medians so a notched tone cannot drag the line, and
outliers dropped by residual. This is the same rule `ap_modem.c` already
follows for the symbol rate: estimate it, do not assume it. A phone, a laptop
and a microcontroller each buffer audio their own way, and an instrument that
believes the nominal timing measures the buffering rather than the loudspeaker.

## Running it

```
sh selftest.sh                       # no sound is emitted
.\run-band-sweep.ps1 -Rig board          # one burst
.\run-band-sweep.ps1 -Rig host-speaker   # one burst
band_sweep slots <capture.wav> <label> --capgain <g> --emitgain <p>
band_sweep minimax <a.tsv> <b.tsv> ...
band_sweep timeline <capture.wav> 5      # what is actually in the recording
```

The rig enforces a **burst budget**. Every emission goes through `Invoke-Burst`,
which throws when the count is exhausted; there is no path to the speaker that
does not. These campaigns run in occupied rooms, and a rig that can emit
unboundedly will.

## Self-test

`selftest.sh` emits no sound and checks the parts that have a known answer: that
a notch at 3000 is avoided, that minimax refuses a tone which is strong on one
path and dead on another (where an average would take it), that a mismatch in
recording settings is reported, and that a synthetic ladder with no channel in
it comes back flat — currently to 0.000 dB across 27 tones, with 63 dB between
each tone and its own leakage.

Two of those cases were wrong when first written and passed for the wrong
reason. The fixtures now carry a note saying so.
